Product

Every stage, in detail — what agents do, what humans decide.

The lifecycle below is not a roadmap. It is the shipped orchestration: 18 stages, 8 agents, 6 human gates, every alternate path handled.

Stage by Stage

Position creation → onboarding

STAGE 1 · REQUISITION & JD

From a three-sentence brief to a budget-checked, market-calibrated JD

The intake step parses the hiring manager’s free-text brief into a structured role specification. The Requisition Agent then verifies headcount budget against the HRIS, fetches the approved compensation band for the level, pulls live market benchmarks, and writes a complete, inclusive job description — proposing a salary ceiling it can defend with data.

Humans decide: the hiring manager approves or red-lines the JD (the agent revises against the feedback, up to a policy limit). If the proposed ceiling exceeds the band, a finance partner must approve the exception before sourcing begins. If the department has no budget, the workflow closes immediately with the reason on record — no human time spent.

STAGE 2 · SOURCING & SCREENING

Top-of-funnel built in minutes, screened in parallel

The Sourcing Agent publishes the posting to the boards it judges relevant, searches the passive talent pool semantically, and writes personalised outreach referencing each candidate’s actual background. As applications land, the Screening Agent fans out — one parallel evaluation per candidate — scoring against an explicit rubric: must-have coverage 50%, experience depth 30%, domain fit 20%.

Humans decide: nothing, for clear-cut cases. But the screener is instructed to flag unconventional-but-promising profiles (the bootcamp grad with a 9k-star open-source project) rather than reject them — and flagged profiles are guaranteed a human review before the shortlist is final. If the qualified pool is thin, an LLM supervisor weighs slate quality against delay and can order another sourcing round.

STAGE 3 · PRE-SCREEN & SCHEDULING

Logistics verified, loops booked — zero email ping-pong

The Pre-screen Agent holds a short chat with each shortlisted candidate: continued interest, notice period, salary expectation, location/work-mode fit. Candidates whose expectations are wildly outside the band exit here — respectfully, and before anyone’s calendar is touched. The Scheduling Agent then reads the panel’s calendars, asks each candidate’s preference once, and books the full loop — reacting to booking conflicts and retrying until every round has a slot.

Humans decide: nothing. This stage is pure coordination — the single biggest source of recruiter toil, fully removed.

STAGE 4 · INTERVIEWS (DURABLE WAIT)

The workflow parks. For as long as humans need.

Interviews are human-paced and Hire doesn’t pretend otherwise. The workflow checkpoints and parks — hours, days or weeks — surviving restarts and redeploys. The moment the final scorecard is submitted (via the approval UI or an ATS webhook), the graph resumes exactly where it stopped. No polling, no cron jobs, no “did anyone chase the panel?”.

STAGE 5 · DECISION & OFFER

An evidence-based decision packet, then a guardrailed close

The Decision-support Agent consolidates every scorecard and screening signal into ranked hire/no-hire recommendations with honest confidence levels — a one-glance decision packet. After the hiring manager makes the call, the Offer Agent negotiates: opens fair, reads the candidate’s counter, revises within the approved ceiling, and closes — typically in minutes.

Humans decide: the hire itself, always. And if the candidate holds out above the approved ceiling, the agent cannot proceed — the tool layer blocks it. The case escalates to a recruiter + finance with the full negotiation log; they approve a new ceiling or hold the line. If a candidate declines, the manager picks a backup from the remaining recommendations — no restart.

STAGE 6 · ONBOARDING

Day-1 readiness from the second the offer is signed

The Onboarding Agent immediately initiates background verification with the vendor, raises ITSM tickets for hardware and access, and schedules a warm pre-boarding email drip across the notice period — the window where most no-shows are lost. The orchestrator then verifies all of it against the systems of record and backfills anything missed.

Humans decide: only BGV discrepancies. A clean report flows straight through; a discrepancy routes to HR/Legal with the vendor’s exact finding for adjudication. A cleared discrepancy completes the hire; a failed one releases the candidate and returns to backup selection.

Human-in-the-Loop

Six gates. Typed contracts. One-glance decisions.

Every gate is a structured payload your approval surface renders — Slack modal, email action, or the built-in inbox — and a typed answer the workflow resumes on. Approvals take seconds because the evidence arrives with the question.

JD approval hiring manager

Full JD draft + proposed comp ceiling + budget summary. Answer: {approved, feedback?}. Rejection routes the feedback straight back into the Requisition Agent for a revision cycle.

Finance exception finance partner

Fires only when the proposed ceiling exceeds the approved band. Answer: {approved, approved_max?}. A rejection doesn’t kill the requisition — it proceeds within band.

Shortlist review hiring manager

Fires only when screening flags unconventional-but-promising profiles. Side-by-side cards: auto-shortlist vs flagged. Answer: {include_ids}.

Interview feedback interview panel

The durable wait. The booked loop is displayed; the workflow resumes when scorecards arrive: {scorecards: [...]} — via UI or ATS webhook.

Hire decision hiring manager

Ranked recommendations with confidence and rationale. Answer: {candidate_id | "none"}. The manager decides; the system never hires autonomously.

Offer exception & BGV adjudication recruiter · finance · HR/legal

Out-of-band offers arrive with the full negotiation log: {approved, new_ceiling?}. BGV discrepancies arrive with the vendor’s exact finding: {cleared, note}.

Always On the Record

The audit trail is the product’s memory.

Every tool call, every agent conclusion, every human approval is an append-only event with a timestamp, a stage, and an actor — agent, human or system. That gives you decision provenance per candidate, cycle-time per stage, your automation-vs-touch ratio, and a measurable agent reliability signal — for free.

It’s also your compliance story: who screened, on what rubric, who reviewed the flags, who made the hire call, who approved the exception.
